# INC-004: Device clock skew causes telemetry rejection

**Type:** Synthetic incident used by EdgeAgentBench.

**Reported symptom:** Downstream telemetry processing stops accepting records at 12:14 UTC.

**Expected root cause:** `PUBLISHER_CLOCK_SKEW`.

## Scenario

Following an edge-device restart, the publisher's system clock is restored from an inaccurate real-time clock (RTC). NTP synchronization has not completed.

The publisher continues sampling and publishing telemetry. Its outgoing messages contain event timestamps approximately 480 seconds behind trusted UTC.

The MQTT consumer receives the messages successfully. However, its freshness policy rejects events older than 120 seconds.

All top-level fixture timestamps represent centrally collected observation times. The event timestamp embedded in a telemetry message represents the publisher's local wall-clock time.

## Required diagnostic evidence

| Evidence ID | Observation | Diagnostic role |
|---|---|---|
| L-033 | A newly sampled event carries the timestamp 12:06:00 UTC | Establishes the event timestamp |
| L-035 | The consumer receives it at 12:14:02 UTC and discards it because its age exceeds 120 seconds | Establishes the rejection mechanism |
| M-038 | Publisher clock offset is -480 seconds | Identifies clock skew |

## Supporting evidence

- L-031: Device restarted and restored its wall clock from RTC.
- L-032: Consumer subscription and freshness policy are active.
- L-034: Broker acknowledged the published message.
- L-036: Publisher is processing live samples without replaying a backlog.
- L-037: Publisher clock is unsynchronized.
- M-035: Consumer continues receiving 120 messages per minute.
- M-036: No messages are accepted downstream.
- M-037: Event-age rejection rate is 120 messages per minute.
- M-039: Publish-to-consume latency remains low.
- M-040: Publisher queue is empty.

The 93% disk utilization in M-031 and L-038 is a distractor. It does not establish a storage failure.

## Expected causal explanation

1. The device restarts with an inaccurate RTC-derived wall clock.
2. NTP synchronization remains incomplete.
3. New telemetry is timestamped approximately eight minutes in the past.
4. MQTT delivery and schema validation succeed.
5. The consumer's 120-second freshness policy discards the messages.
6. No telemetry records are accepted downstream.

## Alternative explanations

- Network delay: inconsistent with the measured 240 ms p95 transport latency.
- Old-message replay: inconsistent with live sampling and an empty publisher queue.
- Broker outage: inconsistent with successful publishes and consumer deliveries.
- Topic mismatch: inconsistent with the publisher topic and consumer subscription.
- Schema incompatibility: the sample passed schema validation.
- Disk exhaustion: local queue persistence remains successful.

## Remaining uncertainty

The data establishes an unsynchronized clock and its downstream effect. It does not independently identify why the RTC contained an incorrect time or why NTP synchronization had not completed.
